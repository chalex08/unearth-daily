# 2026-11-27 (#20, benitoite): judgement calls

- How niche: both critics rated benitoite "enthusiasts only" (rockhounds and Californians who know their state gem). Expect tanzanite to be the most common wrong guess at layer 10.
- A web fact-check found afghanite (blue, transparent, named after Afghanistan, also found in New York) passed the old clue 10, "found in the United States". It now reads "found in California". Afghanite and tanzanite are the layer-9 decoys.
- Clue 2 "never alive": coal, oil, amber, jet, fossils and petrified wood are at depth 1. Limestone and chalk (made from shells) are at depth 3, as rocks. Players may argue either way.
- Clue 4 "gemstone": obsidian, hematite and pyrite are treated as not gemstones (depth 3). Collector stones (celestine, cavansite, afghanite, hauyne, lazulite) count as gemstones.
- Clue 5 "usually blue": topaz, zircon, apatite and fluorite are at depth 4, because they're mostly not blue in nature (blue topaz is usually treated).
- Clue 6 "softer than quartz" (Mohs below 7): kyanite (4.5–7 depending on direction) and blue lace agate (6.5–7) are put at depth 5, on the hard side. Tanzanite (6.5) passes.
- Clue 7 "contains no copper": larimar fails it because its blue comes from copper (confirmed by source).
- Clue 8 "transparent": lapis lazuli and sodalite fail. Lazulite and cavansite can be transparent (depth 8). Afghanite is rarely transparent but passes.
- Clue 9 "named after a place": turquoise ("Turkish stone") and agate (the Achates river) are removed earlier, so their etymology never matters. Celestine (named after the sky) and hauyne (named after Haüy, a person) fail.
- Clue 10 "found in California" could be read as any trace occurrence. Benitoite is the state gem, and California is its only commercial source.
