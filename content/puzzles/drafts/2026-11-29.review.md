- Clue 3 "has at least one stable isotope": bismuth counted as stable; potassium, rubidium, indium etc. count as stable despite trace radioactive isotopes. Promethium is the only lanthanide it removes.
- Clue 2 "solid at room temperature": francium and astatine counted as solid (not directly observed); rutherfordium and heavier (Z 104+) left at layer 1 because their state is unknown. Caesium and gallium counted as solid.
- Clue 5 "ends in -ium": aluminium stops at layer 4 because the US spelling is "aluminum"; caesium/cesium both pass.
- Clue 6 "rare earth metal": counted as scandium, yttrium and the lanthanides (the usual IUPAC definition). Lanthanum drops at layer 4 (name ends -um), promethium at layer 2.
- Clue 7 "named after a place": scandium (Scandinavia), europium (Europe) and thulium (Thule) count; cerium (Ceres) and samarium (a person via a mineral) do not.
- Clue 8 "a place in Sweden": scandium and thulium are not counted as Swedish; yttrium, terbium, erbium and ytterbium (Ytterby) and holmium (Holmia/Stockholm) are.
- Clue 9 "lanthanide": lutetium counted as one; this only removes yttrium at that layer.
- Clue 10 "capital city" relies on knowing Holmia is Stockholm; Ytterby is a village, so the other three drop out.
- Chemical symbols are not accepted as aliases (they collide with ordinary words such as "no", "in", "as").
- Commonness: about 27% common, 40% uncommon, 33% rare at layer 1; the lanthanides are labelled rare.
