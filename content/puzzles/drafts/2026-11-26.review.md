# 2026-11-26 (#19, serpent): judgement calls

- How niche: both critics rated it "enthusiasts only" (early-music and band-history fans). Most adults know the word but not the instrument.
- Critic round 1 found the bass cornett also fit the old clues 9–10 ("finger holes", "low bass notes"). The ladder now ends "plays low bass notes" then "named after an animal". The bass cornett and Russian bassoon are the layer-9 decoys. Round 2 found only the Anaconda (a contrabass serpent), which is an alias of serpent.
- Clue 7 "isn't made of metal": a serpent has a brass crook (the bent tube for the mouthpiece) and often a metal mouthpiece. The body is wood covered in leather, so it's read as "mainly not metal".
- Clue 8 "with a curved body" is vague (all brass curves). It removes only the straight ones: didgeridoo, vuvuzela, bucium, trembita.
- Clue 9 "plays low bass notes" is relative. The lizard (tenor cornett) is put at depth 8; a player could argue it plays low.
- Clue 10 "named after an animal" has a second reading: made from or named for an animal part (shofar, kudu horn, oliphant, bukkehorn). Clue 9 already removed those, so it doesn't matter at layer 10.
- Clue 3 "hold while playing": alphorn, cello, harp and double bass are put at depth 2. Tuba, bassoon and bagpipes count as held.
- Harmonica, saxophone and flute now fail at clue 5 (you don't buzz your lips).
